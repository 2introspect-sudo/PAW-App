Ten trail markers

The core principles of People Are Wild. Every encounter in this app puts one or more of them into practice.

  1. Start with yourself. Observing others is the way in. Your own responses are the real subject.
  2. PAW it. Pause, Assess, Witness. It's not a mantra. It's a method.
  3. Behavior communicates, imperfectly. Words and actions can tell different stories. Notice the camouflage without trying to catch anyone.
  4. The body often reacts first. The nervous system can respond before the mind understands. Activation doesn't automatically mean danger.
  5. The past shapes what you see. Separate what happened, what you interpreted, and what you still don't know.
  6. Understanding isn't excusing. Awareness and boundaries can coexist. Explanation and responsibility can exist together.
  7. Care without carrying. You can stay present with someone without absorbing what isn't yours.
  8. Boundaries support connection. Guilt can mean something is unfamiliar, not that something is wrong.
  9. Notice sooner. Awareness comes first; change grows through new experiences over time.
  10. Listen to all of you. Mind, nervous system, body and heart won't always agree. Get curious about each.

How the app works

Mapping the Wild. The book's five landmarks, each linked to its chapters. Tap a landmark to explore.
Encounters. One story for each landmark, from one connected group of adults. Each walks through PAW: notice your reaction, sort fact from story, then see more than one side.
The Ensemble. Five people whose lives overlap. Each is steady in some places and stuck in others.
Field Guide Prompts. Questions that turn the lens back to you, as at the end of every chapter.
Notes from the Field. Your private reflections. In this prototype they stay in this browser on this device.
Internal Campsite. The tent icon at the top, any time you need to reground.

There are no scores, types or diagnoses here. You won't be told the "right" interpretation; you'll be invited to look at more than one.
